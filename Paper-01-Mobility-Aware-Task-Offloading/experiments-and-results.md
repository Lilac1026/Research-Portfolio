# Experiments and Results — Paper 01

This file records what the authors evaluated and the trends they report. Where the paper presents results as graphs without a complete numerical table, the notes stay qualitative rather than estimating exact values from the plots.

## 1. Evaluation is divided into two stages

The proposed method has a mobility-prediction stage and an offloading stage. The authors therefore select different datasets, baselines, and evaluation outputs for each stage.

## 2. Stage 1 — Mobility prediction

### Dataset

The authors use the **Next Generation Simulation (NGSIM) Vehicle Trajectories and Supporting Data**. The paper describes records containing vehicle position coordinates and longitudinal/lateral velocity components across different time slots.

| Split | Number of samples |
|---|---:|
| Training | 431,204 |
| Evaluation | 32,463 |
| Test | 26,594 |
| **Total** | **490,261** |

### Comparison models

- Transformer — proposed prediction model.
- LSTM — recurrent architecture with gating mechanisms.
- GRU — recurrent architecture with update and reset gates.
- RNN — recurrent sequence model.

### Reported findings

1. **Training loss:** The plotted loss comparison shows the Transformer converging faster and reaching a lower loss than LSTM, GRU, and RNN in the reported experiment.
2. **Position prediction:** The comparison across different vehicle counts shows the Transformer with the smallest prediction error among the four methods.
3. **Safety margin:** The authors note that the prediction error is around the metre scale and use a 5 m safety margin by shrinking the edge-server boundary in advance, to reduce the effect of prediction error on subsequent offloading decisions.

The paper does not provide a complete numerical table of all plotted prediction errors. Do not quote precise per-model values unless you extract and verify them from the original figure or supplementary material.

## 3. Stage 2 — Computation offloading

### Dataset

The authors use a **Synthetic Vehicular 5G Dataset** containing information such as task sizes generated by multiple vehicles, the coordinates of edge servers and UAVs, and computing resources of vehicles, edge servers, and UAVs. Predicted future vehicle trajectories from Stage 1 are also used when evaluating the offloading decisions.

### Comparison methods

- ECMHI — proposed combined method using future vehicle-location information.
- MAPPO — baseline offloading method without the proposed mobility-prediction input.
- COMA — Counterfactual Multi-Agent Policy Gradients.
- MATD3 — Multi-Agent Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient.

### Reported findings

1. **Training reward:** The reward curves begin to converge after roughly 50 episodes. The authors report that ECMHI achieves the highest reward and is more stable after convergence than the comparison methods.
2. **Training delay:** The delay curve for ECMHI is lower than those of the compared methods in the reported training plot.
3. **Training energy:** The paper compares energy consumption during offloading training and reports that ECMHI provides a better overall balance in its experiment.
4. **Different task sizes:** Figures 8 and 9 compare average delay and average energy consumption for task sizes 40, 60, 80, and 100. The authors state that ECMHI has stable performance and a superior overall optimization effect in these tests.
5. **Repeated experiments:** The average delay and energy results are based on more than 50 experiments conducted in the same environment.

The authors specifically discuss the task-size-100 case: ECMHI has lower overall delay, and it also reduces energy consumption effectively while prioritizing delay reduction.

## 4. Experimental environment

## 5. How to describe the results accurately

A defensible summary is:

> In the reported experiments, the Transformer achieved faster loss convergence and lower vehicle-position prediction error than the LSTM, GRU, and RNN baselines. In the second stage, ECMHI achieved higher and more stable training rewards and better delay/energy trends than the compared multi-agent methods across the tested task sizes. These findings are based on the NGSIM prediction evaluation and a synthetic vehicular 5G offloading environment.

Avoid statements such as “the method is proven best for all real-world traffic networks” or quoting exact percentage improvements that are not reported in a numerical table in the paper.

## 6. Questions to ask when reading the plots

- Are the axes and units clear for every metric?
- How much does the prediction error increase as vehicle count increases?
- How does the delay-energy balance change as task size increases?
- Are the improvements consistent across all tested task sizes?
- Would the same trends hold with real network traces and actual UAV flight measurements?
